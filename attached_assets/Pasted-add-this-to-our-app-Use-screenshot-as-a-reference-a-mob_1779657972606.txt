Best family-law visuals
For a family-law case analysis dashboard, the most helpful equivalents would be these:

Graphic type	What it should measure	Why it helps
Timeline line chart	Evidence entries over time, incidents by week/month	Shows escalation, frequency, and sustained patterns.
Stacked bar chart	Incident categories by month: scheduling, communication, safety, co-parenting, education	Helps show what type of issues dominate the case.
Factor coverage chart	Covered vs weak vs missing custody factors by state standard	Makes legal gaps instantly visible.
Response delay chart	Average response times and ignored requests over time	Useful for non-cooperation and communication breakdown analysis.
Pattern frequency chart	Gaslighting, interference, unilateral decisions, threats, manipulation	Shows repeated conduct rather than one-off events.
Parenting-time compliance chart	Ordered exchanges vs completed vs late/cancelled	Strong for custody/order-enforcement cases.
Exhibit strength ranking	Top strongest evidence items by score/confidence/witness support	Helps curate packet and attorney review.
Source breakdown donut	Evidence source mix: texts, emails, notes, photos, school records, medical docs	Shows case diversity and corroboration.
Witness/corroboration chart	Entries with witnesses, documents, or timestamp support	Helps show proof quality, not just quantity.
Court-packet readiness card	Number of exhibits ready, missing labels, missing dates, missing corroboration	Good operational metric for user action.
These are more useful than generic finance-style widgets because they directly support litigation prep: pattern proof, chronology, custody-factor coverage, and corroboration strength.

Best “patterns” to visualize
For family-law case analysis, these are the strongest recurring patterns to visualize:

Missed or changed exchanges.

Late pickups/drop-offs.

Ignored co-parenting requests.

Refusal to share school or medical information.

Hostile or threatening communication spikes.

Unilateral decision-making.

Children being used as messengers.

Interference with parenting time.

Safety-related incidents.

Repeated last-minute cancellations.

A strong dashboard should help the user answer:

Is the behavior isolated or repeated?

Is it getting worse over time?

Which legal factors does it affect?

What evidence is strongest?

What is still missing before court?

Suggested dashboard section
A legal version of this Monarch-style section in your app could look like:

Main chart: “Case Activity Over Time” line chart showing total evidence entries and major incident spikes.

Top-right chart: “Communication & Compliance Trends” combo chart with bars for incidents per month and line for average response delay.

KPI cards: total evidence entries, weak factors, high-risk patterns, packet readiness.

Breakdown chart: horizontal bars for top issue categories.

Feed card: latest evidence entries or recent critical incidents.

Progress cards: factor coverage and court-packet completion.
